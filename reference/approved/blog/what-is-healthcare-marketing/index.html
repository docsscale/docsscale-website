What is healthcare marketing? The kind that fills a schedule

Healthcare marketing is every step between a person needing care and a patient sitting in your chair. Most of what clinics buy covers only the first step. Here is the whole thing, in plain words, with one real clinic.

What is healthcare marketing? It is every step between a person needing care and a patient sitting in your chair: being found, answering, booking, and keeping them. Most of what clinics buy under that name covers only the first step. Your schedule is decided by the other three.

That is the short answer. The rest of this page is the version we would give a practice owner across a desk: what the four jobs are, why the usual purchases do not move the calendar, and what the whole thing looked like at one clinic we work with.

The four jobs healthcare marketing has to do

We run marketing for clinics as one journey with four stages, and we describe it on our how it works page. Every patient you have ever had went through all four, whether anyone was managing them or not.

  1. Attract. Someone with a problem finds you. Ads, Google, your profile on the map, a friend's recommendation.
  2. Capture. They reach out: a call, a form, a message at an odd hour. Something has to catch it.
  3. Convert. The enquiry becomes a booked visit, and the booked visit becomes a person who turns up.
  4. Retain. They come back for the next visit, they leave a review, and they send a friend.

Written out like that it looks obvious. The trouble is that a clinic usually buys each piece from a different person. An ad vendor owns the first stage. A web designer owns part of the second. The front desk owns the third when it has time. Nobody owns the gaps between them, and the gaps are where patients go missing.

What most clinics buy instead

When a practice owner tells us their marketing is not working, it is almost never that nobody can find them. It is one of three things.

An ad agency that reports clicks. The monthly report looks fine. Traffic is up, cost per click is down. But nobody is responsible for what happens after the form is sent, so the report and the schedule disagree.

A website that does not book. It is professional and it loads fast. It also cannot turn a visitor on a phone at nine in the evening into an appointment, because the only way in is a contact form that someone reads the next morning.

Follow-up left to the front desk. The team is already busy with the patients in the building. An enquiry that comes in after hours waits until morning. By then the person has booked with whoever answered first.

None of those is a bad purchase on its own. They are just three quarters of a system with the connecting parts missing. Our services page lists each piece we run, but the point is that they run together.

The best healthcare marketing starts after the click

If you only do one thing after reading this, do this test. Call your own clinic at lunchtime from a number the team will not recognise. Then, tonight, send a message through your own website or your social media page and see when you get a reply.

If the call went to voicemail and the message was answered the next day, you have found your marketing problem, and it is not visibility. Paying to be found more often would send more people to the same closed door.

The fix is unglamorous: every enquiry answered within minutes, any hour, with a reply that offers a time rather than a brochure. A booking link that books. A confirmation straight away and a reminder the day before, because a visit booked from a phone is easy to forget. A message to anyone who missed, offering a new time the same day while they still want it. And, months later, a note to people who have not been in for a while. That last part is the quietest and often the most profitable, which is why we treat patient reactivation as its own service.

What it looks like in one clinic

Central Clinic of Chiropractic in Sumter, South Carolina, has run the whole journey with us since January 2026, and the full story is on our results page. Two chiropractors, private pay, downtown.

The attract stage is Meta and Google ads at around $3k a month, shown to people in and around Sumter. Every click lands on a page built to book. The capture stage is an assistant that answers messages on social media and the website at any hour, and books the consultation itself. Convert is automatic follow-ups until the visit is booked, then confirmations and reminders. Retain is email between visits, review requests after care, and win-back messages to lapsed patients.

Here is the part most marketing pages leave out. The first three months were hard. Patients came in one at a time, and most owners would have stopped. Dr. Jordan did not. From month four, finished patients started sending friends, foot traffic grew, and the ads kept delivering on top. Today the clinic sees 30 or more qualified new patients a month on average, and it is still a client.

In Dr. Jordan's words, as published on that page: "DocsScale runs our ads, our messages and our follow-ups. We just see the patients."

Two things in that story are worth more than the numbers. Nothing in it is a trick. And the system took a season to pay off, not a week.

How to tell if your healthcare marketing is working

Count booked appointments that were kept. Not clicks, not reach, not followers, not a position on a map.

The report we send clients every Monday has four numbers on it: new enquiries, appointments booked, appointments that showed, and what was spent to get them. If something you pay for moves those numbers, keep it. If it only moves the others, it is costing you money without filling the chair.

Then compare that to what a new patient is worth to you over a year. A clinic that knows that figure can decide in a minute whether a campaign is worth running. A clinic that does not know it argues about the ad budget every month.

Where to start this month

  1. Do the lunchtime call and the 9 pm message. Fix whatever you find before anything else.
  2. Make one treatment the focus. The one you want more of and are good at. Give it its own page and, when the after-click part works, its own campaign.
  3. Ask for reviews after every good visit, one tap, and answer every review without confirming anyone is a patient.
  4. Pick four numbers and look at them every Monday. Enquiries, booked, showed, spent.

That is healthcare marketing with the whole journey covered. It is not more complicated than what you are probably buying now. It is just connected.

Common questions

Is healthcare marketing different from marketing any other business?

In two ways. People are choosing someone to trust with their health, so reviews and a fast, human reply matter more than clever adverts. And patient privacy limits what you can say in public: you never confirm that someone is a patient, even in a reply to their own review.

What is the best healthcare marketing for a small clinic?

Fix what happens after someone finds you before you pay to be found more often. Answer every enquiry within minutes, make booking one tap, and remind people before the visit. Then run ads around one treatment you want more of, and ask happy patients for reviews. In that order.

How long does healthcare marketing take to work?

Longer than a month. At the chiropractic clinic in our case study the first three months were slow, and referrals and walk-ins only started stacking on top of the ads from month four. Judge a system over a year.
