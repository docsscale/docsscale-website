Med spa marketing that books consults, not followers

Most med spa marketing advice is about content. The calendar is decided by what happens after someone sends a message. Here is the order to fix things in, with what we saw at a med spa in Las Vegas.

Most med spa marketing advice is about content. Post more reels, show before and afters, get the lighting right. All of that can be true and the calendar can still be half empty, because the calendar is decided somewhere else: in the minutes after someone sends a message.

This is the version of med spa marketing we would do in a spa that has an audience and not enough booked consults. It is short, it is in order, and one of our clients, a med spa in Las Vegas, runs through it as the example, because what happened there is on our results page and it is the clearest picture we have of what actually moves the calendar.

If you only do the first section, do the first section.

Start with the 9 pm test

Here is what the Las Vegas spa looked like when we started, in the words we use on our results page: "Strong Instagram following, weak calendar. DMs answered the next day, if at all. Consult requests leaking to a competitor two blocks away."

Notice what is not in that sentence. Not a bad website, not a small following, not a weak offer. People wanted to book. They sent a message in the evening, nobody answered until the next day, and by then they had booked with the spa two blocks over, which answered first.

So before you touch anything else, run this test tonight. At 9 pm, send your own spa a DM asking about the treatment you most want to sell. Fill in your own website form. Then see what happens, and when. If the honest answer is "tomorrow, if someone remembers", every other idea on this page sends more people to that same silence.

Pick one treatment and give it its own path

A med spa sells a lot of things. Marketing all of them at once produces a feed that looks busy and a campaign that nobody can measure.

In Las Vegas we built one thing first: an injectables consult funnel. One treatment, one page that explains it plainly, one short set of questions, one way to book the consult. The ads pointed at that page and nowhere else.

Pick the treatment that is easiest to say yes to and that brings people back. Give it a page of its own, written the way you would explain it across the desk, with the price range or the way pricing works, who it is for, what a first visit involves, and a button to book the consult. Everything else on the site can wait.

Answer every DM and form within minutes

This is the part that changed the Las Vegas calendar, and it is the least glamorous thing on the page.

Every DM and every form reply within minutes, at 9 pm as well as 9 am. The first reply does not have to be clever. It has to be fast, it has to answer the question that was asked, and it has to offer a time. People choosing a med spa are usually comparing two or three at once, and the one that answers first is the one that gets the consult.

The owner in Las Vegas put it better than we can: "Same followers, same budget. The difference was somebody answering at 9 pm."

If nobody on your team can do that reliably, that is the thing to fix, not the content calendar. It is the whole reason our patient follow-up service exists.

Confirm, remind, and reschedule the same day

A consult booked from a phone at 9 pm is easy to forget by Thursday. Med spa marketing that ignores this fills the calendar with names and then watches a third of them not arrive.

Three steps fix most of it. Confirm the booking straight away, so the person knows it is real. Remind them the day before, in plain words, with the address and how to move it if they need to. And when someone does miss, offer a new time the same day, while they still want the treatment.

In Las Vegas, the reminder sequence with a same-day reschedule for no-shows is a line in the case study. In practice it is the difference between 62 booked consults and 54 people in the chair.

Run ads in your own accounts, one treatment at a time

Once the replies and reminders are in place, ads are worth paying for, because every click now lands on a page that books and a message that gets answered.

Run them in your own ad accounts, not an agency's. You keep the history, the audiences and the results if you ever change who manages them. Advertise one treatment at a time, pointed at that treatment's page, so you can see exactly what a booked consult costs you and switch off what does not work.

The Las Vegas campaigns were Instagram and Meta, for the injectables consult. Not a brand campaign, not a general "book now". One treatment, one page, one number to watch.

Ask for reviews after the visit, carefully

Reviews matter more for a med spa than for most businesses, because people are choosing someone to put a needle in their face. Ask after every good visit, when the person is happy and still in the building, and make it a one-tap link.

Two cautions. Never confirm in a public reply that someone is a client, even if they say so themselves. And never offer anything in return for a review. Both can cost you the listing.

What Las Vegas looked like after one quarter

From our results page, exactly as published: "62 booked consults in the quarter, 54 showed, and a 38% treatment acceptance on first visit." Same spa, same Instagram following, same ad budget as before.

What changed was the order of work. Reply times first, one treatment with its own path, confirmations and reminders, then ads pointed at a page that could book. That order is the method, and it transfers.

Four numbers for Monday

The most useful piece of med spa marketing we do is a short report every Monday with four numbers on it: inquiries, consults booked, consults that showed, and the revenue they brought.

Not followers, not reach, not likes. If a thing you are paying for moves those four numbers, keep it. If it only moves the others, it is costing you time or money without filling the chair.

Where to start this week

  1. Tonight, run the 9 pm test on your own spa and write down how long the reply took.
  2. Pick one treatment and give it its own page and its own consult path.
  3. Put a confirmation, a day-before reminder and a same-day reschedule on every consult.
  4. Only then, run one campaign for that treatment in your own accounts.

Common questions

Does Instagram actually bring med spa clients?

It brings attention, and attention only turns into consults if someone answers quickly. Our Las Vegas client had a strong following and a weak calendar until DMs were answered within minutes instead of the next day. The following did not change. The reply time did.

How much should a med spa spend on marketing?

Work it out from your own numbers rather than a percentage someone quotes. Know what a new client is worth to you over a year, how many more consults you can fit in a week, and what you can afford to pay to win one. Then fix the reply time and the no-shows before you raise the ad budget, because both make every dollar you already spend go further.

Why do so many med spa consults not show up?

Because a consult booked from an ad on a phone is easy to forget, and because the person was probably comparing several providers at once. A confirmation straight away, a reminder the day before, and a new time offered the same day when someone misses do most of the work. In Las Vegas, 54 of 62 booked consults showed once that was in place.
